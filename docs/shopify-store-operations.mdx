# Shopify Store Operations and Growth

**Course ID:** SHP-OPS-201  
**Estimated effort:** 18-22 hours  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Assessment model:** Applied knowledge checks, five practical labs, operational evidence pack and capstone store-readiness review

## Capability outcome

By the end of this course, you will be able to operate a Shopify store through a repeatable, secure and measurable workflow: configure the operating baseline, publish credible products, build collections, validate customer journeys, fulfil orders responsibly, measure performance and make controlled improvements.

## Before you begin

- access to a Shopify development store or an approved training store;
- a named learner account with the minimum permissions needed for products, content, orders and analytics;
- a test product set, test customer identity and non-production payment/shipping configuration;
- a working location for redacted screenshots, checklists and change evidence.

:::warning Production safety
Use a development store or a formally approved change window. Do not place live orders, alter payment settings, publish unreviewed code, expose customer data or install paid applications merely to complete a lab.
:::

## Module 1 - Define the store operating model

### 1.1 Start with the commercial workflow

A Shopify store is not complete when a theme is visible. It must support a reliable flow from product decision to customer discovery, checkout, fulfilment, support, return, reporting and improvement. Document the owner, input, approval and evidence point for each stage.

### 1.2 Separate duties and environments

Keep development, content review and production publishing distinct. The person writing product copy or making a theme change should not automatically be the only person approving it. Use named accounts, least-privilege roles and a defined rollback decision.

### 1.3 Minimum operating controls

Maintain a launch register containing store purpose, target market, product owner, fulfilment owner, support contact, legal-page owner, analytics owner, change reference, approver and next review date.

## Practical Lab 1 - Map the end-to-end commerce workflow

### Scenario

Skunkworks is launching a technology-products storefront that sells physical goods and digital training offers. The team needs a transparent hand-off process before products are made customer-visible.

### Tasks

1. Map the lifecycle from supplier information to product publication, order fulfilment, customer support and post-purchase analysis.
2. Identify the accountable role for each step and at least one independent approval for high-impact changes.
3. Mark points where customer data, financial data, inventory or public claims are introduced.
4. Define a rollback action for a wrong price, unavailable product and incorrect policy link.

### Acceptance criteria

- each activity has an accountable owner;
- customer-visible changes have an approval or review point;
- sensitive data boundaries are clear;
- rollback owners and decision criteria are recorded.

## Module 2 - Build product information that earns trust

### 2.1 Product data as an operational asset

Product titles, descriptions, images, variants, pricing, availability, shipping information and policy links must agree. Treat product data as controlled content, not a once-off upload.

### 2.2 Product-page essentials

Use plain titles, customer-relevant benefits, accurate specifications, variants with clear attributes, high-quality images, availability information and return/support guidance. Do not make claims that cannot be substantiated by the supplier or your own evidence.

### 2.3 Merchandising quality checks

Before publishing, check mobile readability, image crop, alt text, variant selection, price display, stock state, add-to-cart behaviour, search terms and related-product logic.

## Practical Lab 2 - Publish a validated training product

### Tasks

1. Create a draft product in the training store with a title, concise value statement, full description, vendor, product type and tags.
2. Add at least two variants with distinct option names and prices.
3. Add descriptive image alt text and verify images remain legible on mobile.
4. Create a product approval checklist and capture a redacted preview.
5. Have an assessor test product discovery, variant selection and cart addition.

### Acceptance criteria

- no placeholder text or unverified claims remain;
- variants are unambiguous;
- accessibility text describes the image purpose;
- the product remains a draft until approval is recorded.

## Module 3 - Create discoverable collections and navigation

### 3.1 Taxonomy supports search and choice

Collections, menus and filters should reflect how customers decide. A buyer who knows the vendor, a buyer who knows the use case and a buyer who knows the budget may all use different paths to the same product.

### 3.2 Manual and automated collections

Use automated collections where rules are stable and can be tested. Use manual collections for editorial campaigns or curated bundles. Record the rule, owner and review cadence for either type.

### 3.3 Navigation hygiene

Every navigation item needs a clear destination, descriptive label and meaningful order. Remove stale navigation entries. Confirm that footer links include accessible policy, contact and support routes.

## Practical Lab 3 - Design a catalogue discovery path

### Tasks

1. Define three customer intents for the training catalogue.
2. Build one automated and one manual collection supporting those intents.
3. Add the collections to a test navigation menu with customer-centred labels.
4. Test paths from home page, search results, collection page and product page.
5. Record a zero-result search and define the recovery guidance a customer should see.

### Acceptance criteria

- collection rules are testable and documented;
- navigation labels do not rely on internal jargon;
- each path reaches a relevant product within three intentional actions;
- zero-result handling gives customers a useful next step.

## Module 4 - Protect checkout and customer confidence

### 4.1 Checkout is a trust boundary

Customers decide whether the store is credible by its policy clarity, price transparency, contact route, delivery expectation, privacy notice and checkout consistency. Do not add scripts or apps that collect personal data without a clear purpose and review.

### 4.2 Policy and contact controls

Maintain current shipping, return/refund, privacy and terms pages. Ensure the business name, contact method, timing assumptions and exceptions match the actual operating model. Legal review remains an organisation responsibility.

### 4.3 Privacy-by-design practices

Collect only data required to serve the transaction and support request. Restrict access to customer records, use named accounts and export only approved, minimised datasets. Never store payment-card data in course evidence.

## Module 5 - Fulfil, support and resolve exceptions

### 5.1 Order lifecycle discipline

An order should have a clear status, owner and next action. Maintain an exception queue for address failures, stock discrepancies, payment-review cases, delayed dispatches and return requests.

### 5.2 Communication standards

Customer communications should be accurate, timely and non-sensitive. Avoid including credentials, payment details or unnecessary personal information. Use templates with approved tone, escalation rules and target response times.

### 5.3 Returns and refund controls

Apply the published policy consistently, record decision evidence and make sure refund authority is separated from routine support where appropriate.

## Practical Lab 4 - Run an order-exception simulation

### Tasks

1. Create a test order using a test customer and non-production payment method.
2. Simulate a stock or address exception without altering live stock or customer data.
3. Produce a customer-facing update using the approved support template.
4. Record the escalation, approval and final status in an exception register.
5. Identify data that must be redacted before sharing the record with an assessor.

### Acceptance criteria

- the communication states a clear next action and timeframe;
- the register contains owner, timestamp, decision and resolution;
- customer and payment data are not exposed;
- the exception is closed only after the evidence is complete.

## Module 6 - Improve conversion with controlled experiments

### 6.1 Diagnose before changing

Conversion work starts with evidence: search terms, product-page exits, cart abandonment indicators, customer questions and order outcomes. A design preference is not a hypothesis.

### 6.2 Useful hypotheses

A good hypothesis specifies the audience, observed friction, proposed change, expected outcome, metric and guardrail. Example: improving variant labels may reduce selection errors without changing refund rate.

### 6.3 Change control

Test one meaningful change at a time where practical. Capture before/after evidence, approve the release, monitor the outcome and preserve rollback instructions.

## Practical Lab 5 - Create a conversion improvement experiment

### Tasks

1. Inspect training-store product, collection or search behaviour and identify one credible friction point.
2. Write a measurable hypothesis with a primary metric and one guardrail.
3. Create a draft improvement such as a clearer product title, variant label, collection order or call-to-action.
4. Perform mobile and keyboard checks before release.
5. Produce a change record containing approver, rollback step and review date.

### Acceptance criteria

- the proposed change solves a defined customer problem;
- success and safety metrics are measurable;
- the change is reversible;
- evidence distinguishes observation from assumption.

## Module 7 - Measure store health responsibly

### 7.1 Metrics require definitions

Define metrics before reporting them. Common measures include sessions, conversion rate, average order value, product availability, order-to-dispatch time, refund rate, support response time and returning-customer rate. Record the source, calculation and reporting period.

### 7.2 Metric governance

Do not compare inconsistent time periods or sources. Use restricted dashboards for customer-sensitive data. Share aggregated data whenever individual-level detail is not needed for the decision.

### 7.3 Weekly operating review

Review performance, exceptions, top support themes, stock risk, promotional changes, site changes and open risks. Turn decisions into named actions with due dates.

## Module 8 - Launch and operate with a readiness gate

### 8.1 Release readiness

Before a public release, verify the domain, navigation, search, product availability, price/currency display, policy routes, contact route, test checkout, fulfilment handling, analytics consent/configuration and rollback plan.

### 8.2 Post-launch monitoring

Monitor for broken routes, checkout failures, stock anomalies, unexpected traffic changes, support spikes and policy mismatches. Keep a short hypercare window and record decisions.

### 8.3 Escalation boundaries

Escalate payment, privacy, security, legal, inventory and customer-harm concerns to the appropriate owner. Learners must not attempt to remediate production incidents without approval.

## Capstone - Store operations readiness review

### Capstone scenario

You are preparing a Skunkworks Shopify store for a controlled campaign launch. The business has physical products, digital learning offers, a fulfilment partner and a small support team. Leadership requires a release decision supported by evidence, not intuition.

### Deliverables

1. Operating-model and hand-off map.
2. Approved product-page checklist for two representative products.
3. Collection and navigation discovery-path test results.
4. Checkout, privacy and policy readiness review.
5. Order-exception register and customer communication template.
6. Conversion experiment hypothesis and controlled change plan.
7. Weekly dashboard definition with owners and data sources.
8. Go/no-go recommendation, residual risks, rollback owner and next review date.

## Assessment rubric

| Criterion | Weight | Competent evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Store operating model | 15% | Responsibilities, approvals and escalation boundaries are workable |
| Product and discovery quality | 20% | Product data, collections, navigation and accessibility checks are complete |
| Customer trust and privacy | 15% | Policies, contact paths, minimisation and data-handling safeguards are verified |
| Order operations | 15% | Exceptions are owned, communicated and resolved with redacted evidence |
| Measurement and experimentation | 15% | Metrics and hypotheses are defined, ethical and decision-useful |
| Release readiness | 20% | Go/no-go decision, risks, monitoring and rollback are explicit |
