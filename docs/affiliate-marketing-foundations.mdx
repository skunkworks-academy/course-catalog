# Affiliate Marketing Foundations: Platforms, Offers and Trust

**Course ID:** AFF-FND-101  
**Estimated effort:** 6–8 hours  
**Level:** Beginner  
**Assessment model:** Knowledge check, three applied labs and a responsible-launch evidence pack

## Capability outcome

You will be able to explain how affiliate marketing works, assess whether an offer and platform are appropriate for a defined audience, create transparent promotional copy, and set measurable but realistic campaign objectives.

:::warning Income-claim and consumer-protection boundary
Affiliate marketing is a performance-based commercial activity, not guaranteed income. Do not present earnings as typical, conceal a commercial relationship, use deceptive urgency, or make product claims that cannot be substantiated. Follow the rules that apply in the learner's jurisdiction and the platform's terms.
:::

## Module 1 — Understand the value exchange

An affiliate introduces an audience to a merchant's product or service using a tracked link. A commission may be earned when the merchant records a qualifying action under its programme rules. The essential parties are the audience, publisher or affiliate, merchant, platform or network, and any payment or attribution provider.

Success starts with audience value: a relevant problem, a credible recommendation and a clear next action. A link alone is not a strategy.

## Module 2 — Evaluate a platform and offer

Before joining a programme, read its terms. Record eligibility, payment threshold, attribution window, refund or cancellation treatment, geographic restrictions, prohibited traffic sources, brand rules, data handling and support route.

Assess an offer by its customer fit, supplier credibility, price clarity, cancellation terms, support quality and evidence for its claims. A high commission does not compensate for a weak or unsuitable offer.

## Practical Lab 1 — Build an offer evaluation record

1. Choose one training or software offer suitable for a clearly defined audience.
2. Create a one-page evaluation covering audience problem, outcome, exclusions, price, terms, support, evidence and commercial relationship.
3. Identify three claims that require an official source before publication.

**Acceptance criteria:** the offer has a defined audience fit; claims are traceable; exclusions and recurring costs are visible; and the recommendation is not based solely on commission value.

## Module 3 — Design an audience-first content path

Map the path from a useful educational resource to a considered referral. Good entry content answers a real question, names limitations and gives users a choice. Use clear calls to action such as “compare options”, “read the terms” or “start a free trial”, when those actions are accurate.

Avoid manufactured scarcity, hidden conditions and unqualified results statements. Keep a record of the sources used for facts, prices and product features.

## Module 4 — Disclose relationships clearly

A disclosure must be understandable, close to the recommendation and visible before a user acts. It should state that you may receive a commission at no additional cost to the customer where that is true. Do not bury it in a footer, a vague hashtag or a separate policy page.

Privacy notices and consent requirements are separate controls. If you collect email addresses, use a lawful basis, state the purpose, protect the data and honour unsubscribe requests.

## Practical Lab 2 — Create a transparent recommendation

1. Draft a 150–250 word recommendation for the chosen offer.
2. Include a plain-language disclosure, price or pricing caveat, audience-fit statement and one meaningful alternative.
3. Test whether a reader can identify the commercial relationship without clicking a link.

**Acceptance criteria:** disclosure is prominent; factual claims are sourced; limitations are included; and the copy makes no income, health, security or performance claim without evidence.

## Module 5 — Set responsible measurement goals

Start with leading measures you can influence: qualified visits, landing-page engagement, consented subscriptions, click-through rate and support questions. Treat commissions and conversions as outcomes, not promises.

Define the source, timeframe, owner and limitation for each metric. Never use another person's personal data or an affiliate dashboard export as unrestricted marketing material.

## Module 6 — Build a sustainable operating baseline

Maintain an offer register, disclosure template, source register, campaign change log and review cadence. Pause a campaign if facts change, a merchant changes terms, complaints increase or attribution cannot be validated.

## Practical Lab 3 — Prepare a first-campaign brief

Create a brief with audience, problem, approved offer, content asset, disclosure wording, traffic channel, measures, review date, risk owner and stop conditions.

**Acceptance criteria:** every claim has a source; the audience can make an informed choice; data collection is minimised; and someone is accountable for review.

## Capstone — Responsible affiliate launch pack

Submit an offer evaluation record, content path, annotated disclosure, campaign brief and a short explanation of why the campaign benefits the intended audience.

## Assessment rubric

| Criterion | Weight | Competent evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Offer and platform evaluation | 25% | Terms, fit, restrictions and risks are documented |
| Audience value | 20% | Content solves a defined problem before referral |
| Disclosure and claims | 25% | Commercial relationship and limitations are clear |
| Measurement design | 15% | Measures have owners, sources and review dates |
| Evidence quality | 15% | Sources and decisions are reproducible |
