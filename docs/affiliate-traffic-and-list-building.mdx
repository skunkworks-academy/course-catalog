# Affiliate Traffic, Lead Capture and Email Foundations

**Course ID:** AFF-TLF-201  
**Estimated effort:** 8–10 hours  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Assessment model:** Three implementation labs, a journey design and an evidence-led optimisation plan

## Capability outcome

You will be able to plan ethical traffic acquisition, construct a consent-aware landing-page journey, design a useful email sequence and run a controlled experiment without relying on spam, purchased lists or deceptive tactics.

## Module 1 — Choose qualified traffic, not vanity volume

Traffic is qualified when it has a reasonable connection to the audience problem and the next action. Choose channels where you can explain the value exchange: search, educational content, partner communities, webinars, approved paid media or referral partnerships.

Do not buy email lists, scrape contacts, send unsolicited bulk email or use prohibited traffic sources. Check each merchant and platform's traffic policy before launch.

## Module 2 — Map the learner or buyer journey

Use a simple map: intent → useful content → landing page → consented follow-up → considered offer → support or exit. Each stage needs one purpose and one primary measure. Keep alternate paths for people who are not ready to subscribe or buy.

## Practical Lab 1 — Create a channel-to-outcome map

1. Select two permitted channels for a defined audience.
2. Map the content, destination, expected action, tracking method and risk for each.
3. Explain why the channel is relevant rather than merely high-volume.

**Acceptance criteria:** channel policy is checked; the destination matches the promise; and no personal data is collected before it has a stated purpose.

## Module 3 — Build a clear landing-page proposition

A landing page should state who it is for, what it offers, what happens next and what data is requested. Use plain forms, accessible labels, a working privacy link and confirmation messaging. Avoid pre-ticked consent, disguised advertising and claims not supported by the offer owner.

## Module 4 — Operate permission-based email

Email works when subscribers expect it and can control it. Capture consent records where required, identify the sender, include an unsubscribe mechanism, honour preferences promptly and keep lists secure.

Build sequences around education: onboarding, problem framing, comparison, practical guidance and an optional recommendation. Frequency should be deliberate and reviewable.

## Practical Lab 2 — Draft a consent-aware sequence

1. Create an opt-in proposition and form fields limited to what is needed.
2. Draft three messages: welcome, practical learning value and transparent recommendation.
3. Add sender identity, unsubscribe language and a measurement plan.

**Acceptance criteria:** consent is not assumed; the commercial relationship is disclosed; and the sequence remains useful even if the recipient never purchases.

## Module 5 — Measure the journey safely

Use event names such as landing-page view, consented subscription, email open, click and conversion only where legally and technically appropriate. Define consent mode, retention period, access roles and reporting aggregation before collecting data.

Attribution is imperfect. Use it to guide investigation, not to overstate causation or individual behaviour.

## Module 6 — Improve through controlled experiments

Write one hypothesis at a time: audience, friction, proposed change, expected measure and guardrail. For example, a clearer form explanation may improve completed subscriptions without increasing unsubscribe or complaint rates.

## Practical Lab 3 — Produce an experiment card

Create an experiment card for a landing page or email: baseline, hypothesis, change, primary measure, guardrail, sample or duration, owner, rollback and review date.

## Capstone — Permission-based audience-growth plan

Submit a journey map, landing-page outline, three-message sequence, consent-and-data checklist and experiment card. Include a decision explaining why each channel and data field is necessary.
